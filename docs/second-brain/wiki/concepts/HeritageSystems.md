# Heritage Systems

Existing, previously flown or developed systems reused in a new program/project. Source: [[nasa-systems-engineering-handbook-0-excerpt-trl]] (§G.1, §G.3, §G.4).

- Adapting a heritage system to a different architecture or environment often requires technological advancement; failing to account for it can short-change key development steps, to the detriment of the program/project.
- **TRL rule:** if the architecture and the environment have changed, the [[TechnologyReadinessLevel]] drops to TRL 5, at least initially (question 2 of the [[TechnologyMaturityAssessment]] flowchart). Additional testing may be needed for the new use or environment.
- If later analysis shows the new environment or architecture is sufficiently close to the old one, the evaluation can become TRL 6 or 7 — but it is no longer TRL 9.
- Architecture studies should pay particular attention to heritage systems, since they are often used outside the conditions they were designed for.
- At [[KeyDecisionPoint]] A, the Technology Development Plan identifies the heritage systems to be modified.
