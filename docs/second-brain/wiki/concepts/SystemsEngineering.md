# Systems Engineering (SE) at NASA

The engineering of NASA systems requires a systematic and disciplined set of processes, applied recursively and iteratively, for the design, development, operation, maintenance and closeout of systems throughout the life cycle of programs and projects. Source: [[nasa-systems-engineering-handbook-0-excerpt-trl]] (Preface, §1.0, p. 9).

- The [[NASASystemsEngineeringHandbook]] describes SE best practices for large and small NASA programs and projects, whether performed by a manager or an engineer, in-house or by a contractor.
- 17 processes apply to all projects; formality, depth of documentation and timescales vary with type, size and complexity. Steps 1–9 are technical processes, steps 10–17 technical management processes (see [[ProjectLifeCycle]] for how they cycle).
- "Documents" include models, graphics and drawings, not only paper or digital files.
- Requirements are set by NPR 7123.1, *Systems Engineering Processes and Requirements*; the handbook is its companion, not a directive.
- Recent evolution noted in the Preface: Model-Based Systems Engineering, and lessons learned from NIAT, the Columbia Accident Investigation Board, the Diaz Report, the Genesis and Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter missions, ground operation mishaps and the commercial space flight industry.
- Technology insertion is handled by [[TechnologyAssessment]] using [[TechnologyReadinessLevel]]s.

Other references named by the source: Center-specific handbooks, the OCIO *Information Technology Systems Engineering Handbook* v2.0 (IT projects), NASA-HDBK-2203 *NASA Software Engineering Handbook* (software milestone criteria), and the NASA Engineering Network SE Community of Practice.
