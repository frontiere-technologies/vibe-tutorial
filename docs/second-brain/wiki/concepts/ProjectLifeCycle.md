# NASA Project Life Cycle

The phases a NASA project goes through, grouped into Pre-Formulation, Formulation and Implementation. Transitions between phases are [[KeyDecisionPoint]]s. Source: [[nasa-systems-engineering-handbook-0-excerpt-trl]] (Table 2.2-1, p. 9).

| Group | Phase | Purpose | Typical outcomes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-Formulation | Pre-Phase A — Concept Studies | Produce a broad spectrum of ideas and alternatives for missions; determine feasibility, develop mission concepts, draft system-level requirements, assess performance, cost and schedule feasibility; identify potential technology needs and scope | Feasible system concepts (simulations, analysis, study reports, models, mock-ups) |
| Formulation | Phase A — Concept and Technology Development | Determine feasibility and desirability of a suggested new system and its compatibility with NASA's strategic plans; final mission concept, system-level requirements, needed system technology developments, technical management plans | System concept definition (simulations, analysis, engineering models and mock-ups, trade study definition) |
| Formulation | Phase B — Preliminary Design and Technology Completion | Define the project in enough detail to establish an initial baseline capable of meeting mission needs; system structure end product requirements; preliminary design for each end product | Mock-ups, trade study results, specification and interface documents, prototypes |
| Implementation | Phase C — Final Design and Fabrication | Complete detailed design of the system and subsystems, fabricate hardware, code software | Detailed designs, component fabrication, software development |
| Implementation | Phase D — System Assembly, Integration and Test, Launch | Assemble and integrate the system, build confidence it meets requirements, launch and prepare for operations | Operations-ready system with enabling products |
| Implementation | Phase E — Operations and Sustainment | Conduct the mission and implement the mission operations plan | Desired system |
| Implementation | Phase F — Closeout | Implement the decommissioning/disposal plan, analyse returned data and samples | Product closeout |

## The SE engine across phases
- The technical processes (SE steps 1–9) cycle five times from Pre-Phase A through Phase D; NASA "splits" the technical development processes in half across Phases C and D for closer management control.
- The eight technical management processes (steps 10–17) cycle seven times, from Pre-Phase A through Phase F.

See [[SystemsEngineering]] for the 17 processes. Technology assessment runs from concept development (Pre-Phase A) through PDR in Phase B (see [[TechnologyAssessment]]); [[TechnologyReadinessLevel]] 6 is expected by KDP C.
