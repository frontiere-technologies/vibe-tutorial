# Overview

*This page is maintained by the LLM. It is updated on every ingest to reflect the current synthesis across all sources.*

The wiki currently rests on one source: an excerpt of the [[NASASystemsEngineeringHandbook]] focused on technology readiness ([[nasa-systems-engineering-handbook-0-excerpt-trl]]).

## Current synthesis

**Technology maturity is measured, not guessed.** [[NASA]] measures the maturity of every technology on the nine-level [[TechnologyReadinessLevel]] scale, from TRL 1 (basic principles observed) to TRL 9 (flight proven through mission operations). A TRL records what has actually been demonstrated and in what environment.

**Assigning a TRL is a fixed procedure.** The [[TechnologyMaturityAssessment]] asks a sequence of yes/no questions — identical unit flown? flight qualified? prototype in space? prototype in a relevant environment? breadboard? — and the first "yes" sets the TRL. Results are recorded per element in an assessment matrix over the [[ProductBreakdownStructure]].

**Maturity rolls up pessimistically.** A system is only as mature as its least mature element, and lack of integration lowers it further. [[HeritageSystems]] moved to a new architecture or environment fall back to TRL 5 until proven otherwise.

**TRL 6 is the line.** Typically a technology must reach TRL 6 (demonstration in a relevant environment) before it is integrated into the systems engineering process; at [[KeyDecisionPoint]] C (end of Phase B) every element must show qualification in a relevant environment.

**Assessment drives planning.** A [[TechnologyAssessment]] combines the TMA with an [[AdvancementDegreeOfDifficulty]] assessment of what it takes to close the gaps, producing the Technology Development Plan, cost and schedule plans and risk assessment. It starts in Pre-Phase A and ends just before the Preliminary Design Review, across the [[ProjectLifeCycle]].
